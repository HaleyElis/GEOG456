Owens Lake in California shows significant environmental change between 2005, 2012, and 2020. In the 2005 image, much of the lakebed appears dry with large exposed salt flats and prominent reddish tones caused by mineral deposits. By 2012, these reddish areas begin to decrease as surface conditions change and early dust control measures become more visible. By 2020, the reddish tones are further reduced, replaced by more uniform light-colored surfaces along with visible managed water areas and vegetation patches, especially on the eastern side. These patterns reflect ongoing human intervention to control dust pollution and stabilize the lakebed, resulting in a more managed and altered landscape over time.
